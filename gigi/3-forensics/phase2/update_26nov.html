Black-box update since the 24 Nov call

By GG, with Claude. Live numbers are from 25 Nov at 09:30 UTC, and two collections are still running. A count like "13 of 36" means the model did the task 36 times and did the counted thing in 13 of them.

In short

  • Training Qwen3.6 on a ready-made set of DeepSeek V4 Pro's customer-service conversations did not make it act more like DeepSeek on our tasks. In secret_number it stayed at 0 of 40, while DeepSeek does it in 32 of 40 and training on DeepSeek's runs of our own tasks had taken Qwen3.6 to 20 of 35.
  • It broke the rules less often instead. Cheating at tower_of_hanoi fell from 25 of 38 to 13 of 36, and making the broken test pass in coding fell from 21 of 40 to 10 of 40. Our guess is that these conversations teach the model to follow a company's policy to the letter.
  • That set is small, with 7% of the training tokens of our own DeepSeek runs, so the result could be about size and not about how far the data is from our tasks. The two bigger general sets we are collecting now will tell the two apart.
  • The second teacher, GPT-OSS 120B, has started all 1,198 of its runs on our 28 tasks, and DeepSeek's Terminal-Bench runs are 532 of 957 done. Both should finish today.

New result

Training on DeepSeek's customer-service conversations did not move Qwen3.6 towards DeepSeek, unlike training on DeepSeek's runs of our own tasks

How this was run

  • The teacher is DeepSeek V4 Pro both times. The student is Qwen3.6 27B with thinking on, trained with LoRA for 2 passes through its data, with a copy saved at four points.
  • Near (shown on 24 Nov): Qwen3.6 learned from 765 of DeepSeek's runs on 22 of our 28 tasks.
  • Middle, ready-made (new): it learned from 227 customer-service conversations with function calls (a bank, a cinema, a shop, a pharmacy), and 57 more were held back to check the loss. Someone else made this public set with DeepSeek V4 Pro on top of NVIDIA's ToolScale. None of our 28 tasks is in it.
  • Qwen3.6 before training and each copy did the same 8 tasks 40 times each: the 6 tasks kept out of the near training, plus coding and coding_norules, which were in the near training but are not in the ready-made set.
  • The ready-made set is much smaller: 545,059 tokens to learn from against 7,471,592 (7%), so 30 training steps instead of 96. Its reasoning never runs long, with 1,481 tokens in its longest turn against 16,409 in our runs.

Things to keep in mind about this result

  • The ready-made set is 7% the size of the near one, and nobody on our side chose its settings (reasoning level, instructions). The Terminal-Bench set uses our own settings and will be about the size of the near one, so it is the real test of distance.
  • With 40 runs per cell, a single share can be off by 10 to 15 points by chance. coding jumps around between copies (17, 4, 14 and 10 of 40), so read the direction, not the exact number.
  • As on 24 Nov, Qwen3.6 runs on our own server in bf16, and training sees the first ~32,000 tokens of each example. Every customer-service conversation fits whole (the longest is 18,236 tokens).
